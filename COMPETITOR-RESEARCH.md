# PRO1ST — Competitor Research & Rebrand Direction

Research pass across the professional-audio category, to establish what the
market actually does and where PRO1ST should sit.

**Method:** 15 competitor sites analysed structurally; 2 viewed directly in a
browser for visual and motion language. 2 blocked automated access
(Allen & Heath, Audio-Technica — 403) and 1 domain no longer resolves
(Studiomaster India).

---

## Sites analysed

**Global tier-1 (the benchmark)**
Shure · Sennheiser · JBL Professional · Electro-Voice · RCF · QSC ·
d&b audiotechnik · L-Acoustics · Genelec · ADAM Audio · Pioneer DJ /
AlphaTheta · Mackie · Behringer · Wharfedale Pro

**India / direct competition**
Ahuja Radios · 5 Core

---

## The single most important finding

**Every serious competitor leads with a product. None of them lead with a
manifesto.**

RCF — the closest structural peer to PRO1ST — opens with a full-bleed macro
photograph of a speaker cabinet, the product name in large type, and one
outlined CTA: *"Discover the series."* No brand essay, no scroll-jacking, no
3D. Within one scroll you are looking at a 3-up grid of product families, then
a 4-up grid of more products.

The current PRO1ST homepage does the opposite. Products appear on roughly the
sixth screen, behind an exploded-chassis animation, a brand-origin essay and a
signal-chain walkthrough. It is an art piece with a shop attached.

That is the rebrand.

## Patterns common to 13+ of 15 sites

| # | Pattern | Seen at |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero is a single product, big photo, product name, one CTA | RCF, JBL, Sennheiser, Mackie, ADAM, Pioneer, Behringer, EV |
| 2 | Product category grid within the first two screens | Shure, RCF, Behringer, Genelec, Mackie, EV |
| 3 | Featured / new / best-seller product cards on the homepage | Shure, Sennheiser, RCF, Mackie, Wharfedale |
| 4 | Case studies and real installations as the main social proof | L-Acoustics, d&b, RCF, JBL, ADAM, Wharfedale |
| 5 | "Where to buy" / dealer locator in the primary navigation | RCF, Genelec, Mackie, Shure, EV |
| 6 | Dark palette with product photography as the light source | RCF, JBL, EV, Sennheiser, Mackie, Pioneer, ADAM |
| 7 | Newsletter capture near the footer | Shure, EV, ADAM, Sennheiser, L-Acoustics |
| 8 | Multi-column footer led by product categories | All 15 |
| 9 | Minimal body copy — a name and one descriptive line per tile | RCF, JBL, Mackie, Behringer |
| 10 | Applications / use-case segmentation as a browse path | RCF, Pioneer, Mackie, Ahuja, Genelec |
| 11 | Trust strip immediately under the hero | RCF (events/academy), Shure (shipping/returns/support) |
| 12 | Awards and certification badges | Genelec, ADAM, L-Acoustics, Sennheiser, RCF (EN54) |

## Patterns almost nobody uses

- **Scroll-jacking / pinned scroll sequences** — not one of the 15.
- **Pseudo-3D product illusions** — none. They use photography.
- **Long brand manifestos on the homepage** — only d&b, and it is a
  single line, not a screen.
- **Preload curtains** — none.

This matters: the current site uses all four.

## India-specific findings

**Ahuja Radios** is the incumbent and the market leader. Established 1940,
"eight decades," a national dealer map, and a very wide catalogue. Its site
runs on table layouts with no modern typography — it is credible but visually
a decade behind.

**5 Core** is Shopify and converts hard: discount badges, "up to 50% off",
sold-out scarcity markers, worldwide-delivery and 30-day-return trust badges,
a visible phone number, and bundles.

**The opening for PRO1ST:** Ahuja owns trust but looks dated. 5 Core converts
but looks cheap. Nobody in the Indian market pairs tier-1 international visual
quality with local trade credibility. That is the position to take.

Also note: Ahuja puts a **WhatsApp enquiry popup with state selection** front
and centre. WhatsApp-first is correct for this market, which validates the
enquiry change already made.

## CTA language actually used

| Purpose | Wording competitors use |
|---|---|
| Product discovery | "Discover the series", "Learn more", "Explore", "See more" |
| Purchase | "Buy now", "Add to cart", "Shop now" |
| Trade | "Where to buy", "Find a dealer" |
| Support | "Contact our support team" |
| Content | "Read article", "Watch keynote" |

Nobody writes "Enter the arsenal". Distinctive, but it does not tell a buyer
what happens next — and category CTAs are conventional for a reason.

---

## Recommended direction

**Keep:** the dark industrial palette, the signal orange, the typographic
system, the technical mono detailing. These are already at or above tier-1
standard and are the brand's genuine asset.

**Change:** the information order and the ratio of art to product.

### Homepage running order

| Position | Section | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero — flagship product, name, price, two CTAs | Pattern 1 |
| 2 | Trust strip | Pattern 11 |
| 3 | Shop the range — category grid | Pattern 2 |
| 4 | Featured products | Pattern 3 |
| 5 | Product gallery wall | Pattern 9 |
| 6 | The chain — system story | Differentiator, but after products |
| 7 | The build — engineering proof | Differentiator, kept shorter |
| 8 | Origin + figures | Pattern 4 |
| 9 | Craft | Supports 4 |
| 10 | Manifesto | Brand close, not brand open |
| 11 | Trade / dealer CTA | Conversion |
| 12 | FAQ + showroom + map | Support |

### Still missing versus the benchmark

1. **Case studies / real installations** — the single most common social proof
   in the category, and PRO1ST has none. Needs client photography and consent.
2. **Dealer locator / "Where to buy"** — in primary nav at 5 of 15.
3. **Awards, certifications, test standards** — EN54-type badges carry real
   weight in this category.
4. **Applications / use-case browse path** — "For weddings", "For venues",
   "For rental stock".
5. **Ratings and reviews** — 5 Core uses them; Shopify supports them.
